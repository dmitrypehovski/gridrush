GRIDRUSH

demo guide — what each page shows, what each panel means, and the order to show them

This page is for a reader who has two minutes and no context. Every other page in this project answers one question; this page says which one, and what to look at first. Everything is offline, needs no keys, and every number is reproducible from the repository.

1 · the one-sentence story

A data centre can be built in 2–3 years, but connecting it to the grid takes 7–10 years. On stage the utility was asked what a 98%-availability contract would change, and answered "we're trying". GRIDRUSH answers that question with a computed plan: which edge sites to connect, at what MW, under which availability tier and with what flexibility — verified over all 8,760 hours of 2025 and a real AC power flow.

IN LOOPPhysics inside the optimiser dashboard_physics.html · 2.39×

The same dashboard layout, but the loop ran with a measured admission limit of 6.53 MW of new load per feeder as a hard constraint inside the portfolio builder — and the final plan is verified with eight real AC power-flow runs, all clean.

Why 6.53 and not 8.2? They answer different questions. 8.2 MW is the first thermal violation when the top-5 profile is scaled uniformly; 6.53 MW is the conservative limit we enforce: the same total spread over the five best-headroom buses in proportion to their headroom, with every bus at or above 0.95785 p.u. and every branch at or below 100%.

What to look at: the headline drops from 4.30× to 2.39× ($279.9M → $155.5M, 85.4 → 44.7 MW). That −44.5% is the honest cost of real physics — say the number before a judge asks.

REALReal PG&E substations dashboard_real.html · 1.22×

The site model rebuilt on the public PG&E GRIP Data Portal (ArcGIS REST, no key): eight real substations with real coordinates, facility ratings and 2026 loadings — e.g. SAN RAMON 302.2/136.9 MW, STOCKDALE 327.1/275.2, CLAYTON 193.1/124.9.

Result: 1.22×, 397.7 MW at month 12, 11,533 contract-compliant MW-months, zero violations.

Why so much lower — and why this is the most valuable finding: at the substation aggregate level, real PG&E substations are large and are not the bottleneck, so flexibility buys little. The constraint lives at the circuit level: in a sample of 2,000 circuits, 124 had a positive 2026 deficiency (538 rows are a "no data" sentinel, so the honest share is 124 of 1,462 circuits with data = 8.5%). That is exactly the data GRIDRUSH asks for.

5 · honest caveats (say them before a judge does)

1. "Zero modelled violations" is structural for flexible service: flexible load is clipped to headroom by construction. The constraint that actually binds is each site's SLA, enforced with zero breach energy. Violations would matter for a firm-heavy portfolio.

2. The ratio is invariant to the price level (4.30× at ⅓×, 1× and 3× margin), and the physical ratio 3.82× contains no prices at all. Flattening the SLA premium and doubling the penalty still gives 4.04×. What is assumption-dependent is the absolute dollar level, not the comparison.

3. The "all firm" baseline is honest but weak (651 MW-months at 200 MW of nameplate) because firm capacity is capped by the substation's tightest hour. That is precisely why the real-PG&E result (1.22×) matters.
