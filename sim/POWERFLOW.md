# AC power flow — measured on a real medium-voltage feeder

**What was checked.** One measured AC power-flow study on `pandapower.networks.mv_oberrhein`: a real German 20 kV MV network, **179 buses, 181 lines, two 110/20 kV, 25 MVA transformers**. Base case: **37.1 MW served** (61.9 MW nameplate × 0.6 load scaling in the public case), converged, **0 violations**, **min V = 0.9756 p.u.** (bus 190), **max loading = 85.5%** (transformer T142; T114 at 70.9%). One AC PF takes ~16 ms.
**Per-bus headroom** (largest single-bus added load with 0 violations, 15 load-centre buses): **2.84–6.60 MW** — transformer-limited on strong buses, voltage-limited on weak ones. Headroom is not additive: the two 25 MVA transformers set a feeder-wide spare capacity of **≈8.2 MW**.
**Our load class.** The five largest connected sites in `results/loop.json` total **31.8 MW** (8.5 / 7.0 / 5.75 / 5.5 / 5.0 MW). On the five best-headroom feeder buses (best-case siting) the AC PF converges only to a violated state: **71 violations, min V = 0.921 p.u. (bus 190), max loading = 159.5%**. At scale 1.5 = **47.6 MW**: **137 violations, min V = 0.8839 p.u. (bus 190), max loading = 209.6%**; min V crosses 0.90 p.u. (physical voltage collapse) at **41.2 MW**.
**What passes.** **8.2 MW** (0.258× the top-5 portfolio) is the largest tested load with converged AC PF and **0 violations** (min V 0.9635, max loading 99.99%); the first violation at **8.4 MW** is transformer T142 reaching **100.4%**. A single **4.5 MW** site fits at bus 73 with 0 violations and 90.6% loading: the per-site load class is physically real for a 20 kV feeder — five of them on one feeder are not.
**Caveat (honest).** `mv_oberrhein` is a real feeder, but assigning our synthetic sites to its buses illustrates the **load class**, not a claim about a specific project site. The two transformers, not the 20 kV line ratings, are the binding constraint. Portfolio-dependent numbers are a snapshot of `results/loop.json` (sha256 prefix in `results/powerflow.json → portfolio.sha256`); rerun `sim/powerflow.py` after the agent loop changes the portfolio.
**Reproduce.** `<spike-grid>/.venv/bin/python sim/powerflow.py` → `results/powerflow.json` + `web/physics.html`. The full study takes **≈8 s** (bisection over 15 buses is most of it); one portfolio check is 16–20 ms.
